THE COURSE

A research course.
With the code to back it up.

A research and practice course on learning from connected data. Begin with graph theory, random walks, and PageRank; move through DeepWalk, node2vec, graph convolution, attention, adversarial learning, and graph transformers. Read critically, reproduce results, and develop an original, carefully evaluated research project.

You will move between mathematical ideas, working code, and research discussions. Follow a method from its assumptions to its results—and test the claims yourself.

ASSESSMENT

Work that adds up.

Practical research and individual understanding. These proposed weights total 100% and will be confirmed before the course begins.

25105152520
  • 25%

    Paper portfolio

    Paper studies per group. For each: understanding and critique 20%, implementation and reproducibility 40%, evaluation 20%, presentation and individual defense 20%.

  • 10%

    Surprise quizzes

    Short individual quizzes on foundations, assigned readings, and implementation reasoning. Timing is unannounced; scope follows material already assigned.

  • 5%

    Research participation

    Prepared discussion, constructive peer reviews, and evidence-based questions. Attendance alone earns no separate marks.

  • 15%

    Midterm examination

    Individual examination on weeks 1–7; preferred timing is week 8, with the exact date to be announced. Duration: 90 minutes (tentative). Concepts, derivations, graph problems, and interpretation of experiments.

  • 25%

    Phased research project

    Proposal 3%, reproducible baseline 5%, method and pilot 5%, full evaluation and demo 5%, individual research paper 7%.

  • 20%

    Final examination

    Individual cumulative examination with emphasis on post-midterm material, during the official final examination period. Duration: 120 minutes (tentative).

No separate graded homework or attendance component. Practical exercises support the paper portfolio and project. Any reuse between the two must be declared and must add a distinct contribution to receive credit. Examination aids, exact dates, late-work rules, and quiz make-up arrangements will be announced as per university policy. Shared work is accompanied by individual contribution statements and oral checks.

How this collection was selected+

Author coverage comes first: include the publicly verifiable graph-learning papers, graph descriptors, graph-learning applications, and directly relevant graph-distinguishability foundations coauthored by Mudassir Shabbir. Coverage was checked on 2 November 2026; this does not assert coverage of unpublished or unlisted manuscripts.

04 / THE RESEARCH STUDIO

A question of your own.

Work from a testable hypothesis to reproducible evidence. Every student writes and submits an individual research paper.

THE OUTPUT

A working research artifact.
A paper you can defend.

6–8 pages per student
References & appendices excluded

Five phases. One sustained investigation.

  1. W3

    Question and proposal

    3%

    A two-page proposal: research question, hypothesis, related papers, dataset access and license, metrics, compute budget, and risks.

  2. W6

    Reproducible baseline

    5%

    Data audit, frozen splits, simple and competitive baselines, runnable environment, and initial results.

  3. W10

    Method and pilot

    5%

    A justified extension or diagnostic, pilot experiments, implementation details, and a revised experiment plan.

  4. W13

    Evaluation and demo

    5%

    Ablations, multiple seeds where feasible, uncertainty, runtime and memory measurements, error analysis, and a working demonstration.

  5. W15

    Individual research paper

    7%

    Each student submits an individually authored 6–8 page research paper, excluding references and appendices, plus an artifact link and contribution statement. Shared project results must be attributed; individual analysis and writing are required.

Research standard, paper structure & project rubric+

A rigorous replication with a meaningful extension, a careful negative result, or a focused new method can all make a strong project. Publication acceptance is not a grading criterion. The required paper is submitted to the course; external conference or journal submission is optional and requires agreement among the authors.

Paper structure. Abstract; introduction and question; related work; method; experimental setup; results and ablations; limitations and ethical considerations; conclusion; references; reproducibility and individual contributions.

Across the project milestones, assess clarity of question, technical correctness, strength of baselines and evaluation, reproducibility, and quality of argument. The individual paper is assessed for synthesis and writing as well as the student's documented contribution; novelty alone does not determine the grade.

THE FIVE-PAPER PORTFOLIO

Understand it. Implement it. Present it.

Normally in pairs, each group studies at least five distinct papers. Both partners must be able to explain and defend the work.

  1. 01

    Groups will normally have two students. Each group must understand, implement, evaluate, and present at least five distinct papers from the list. Register choices early so the class covers the full list; the instructor fills coverage gaps and approves overlap.

  2. 02

    Choose at least one random-walk or spectral paper, one message-passing or attention paper, one advanced paper, one evaluation or reliability paper, and one paper aligned with the project. For a theoretical or benchmark paper, implement its central diagnostic, counterexample, or evaluation protocol.

  3. 03

    Each study includes a concise critical brief, runnable code with a documented environment, a reproduced central result, one controlled ablation or stress test, an experiment log, and a presentation with questions. Small-scale reproductions are acceptable when the scope and departures are declared.

  4. 04

    Both partners must understand every selected paper and contribute to implementation and presentation. Use contribution statements and brief individual oral checks. Cite reused code and distinguish the group's implementation from upstream code.

  5. 05

    Proposed portfolio checkpoints are weeks 4, 6, 9, 11, and 13. Each checkpoint includes a short live presentation in class or a parallel studio. Recorded full explanations and rotating live discussions keep the workload feasible as enrollment grows.

What counts as a reproducible study?+

Use fixed train/validation/test splits; select models on validation data and reserve the test set for final evaluation. Document preprocessing, negative sampling, random seeds, hardware, and runtime. Report all attempted conditions relevant to the research claim.

Cite papers, datasets, code, and any AI assistance. Students remain responsible for correctness and must be able to explain and reproduce submitted work. Do not fabricate results or submit generated prose as an individually authored research argument without disclosure and instructor guidance.

Follow dataset licenses and access conditions. Avoid collecting personal data for class projects; use public benchmark or synthetic data with an appropriate research purpose. Do not upload restricted data or credentials to public repositories.
